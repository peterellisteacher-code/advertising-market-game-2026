# Independent visual review: Advertising Market creative studio

Review the supplied production screenshots independently and adversarially. They show the same browser-game creative studio at 1366×768 and 1920×1080. Do not assume the implementation is good or bad, and do not infer a preferred verdict from the evidence.

## Product context

- Audience: Australian Year 10 English students working in pairs on one computer for 50–60 minutes.
- Experience: a fun advertising-market game, not an assignment portal. Students invent a product, create its advertising, then enter a live class market.
- Current screen: the Assets tool in a Canva-like but deliberately bounded creative studio.
- The left rail switches among Build, Assets, Words, Logo, Image, and Price.
- The drawer exposes a searchable offline catalogue: 1,125 product shells by default, plus separate Parts and All views. Each catalogue item has an image, name, and dollar cost. Students can recolour and place pieces on the live advertising canvas.
- The Image tool contains an optional low-cost fal.ai Object Forge and a later reference-to-image “Make It Real” route; the offline catalogue must remain a strong default.
- Product-shell art is deliberately sparse, neutral, unbranded raster line art so students can personalise it.

## Acceptance constraints

- The interface must be usable without text clipping, orphaned labels, hidden essential controls, or ambiguous focus at both supplied resolutions.
- The canvas must remain large enough for genuine composition while the asset drawer remains useful.
- Controls and language should be clear to 14–16-year-olds, with comfortable classroom click targets and legible contrast.
- The visual identity should feel energetic, contemporary, and relevant to advertising/creative work without imitating a school worksheet.
- The catalogue must support fast browsing across a very large inventory without visually overwhelming students.
- Evaluate the screenshots in relation to strong browser game editors and bounded Canva-like creative tools, while respecting that this is a classroom game and not a full professional design suite.

## Review task

Identify the most important visual and interaction-design findings that can be established from the screenshots. Assess composition, hierarchy, use of space, drawer/canvas balance, typography, text containment, card density, discoverability, state clarity, accessibility, and visual coherence across resolutions. Distinguish blockers from polish. Return a decisive synthesis with a terminal verdict of PASS, PASS WITH FIXES, or FAIL, and a short prioritised list of concrete changes. Do not invent implementation facts that are not visible.
