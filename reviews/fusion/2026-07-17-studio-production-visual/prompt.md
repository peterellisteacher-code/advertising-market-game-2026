# Independent visual release review: Advertising Market Studio

Review the two supplied screenshots of the exact assembled production build at their original resolutions. Act as an adversarial visual/product-design panel. Determine whether the Studio is release-ready for its stated classroom use, and provide a decisive redesign brief if it is not.

## Context

- Audience: Year 10 English students working in pairs on one classroom computer.
- Session: 50–60 minutes.
- Experience: a fun, content-focused advertising game; it must not use assignment/unit framing or feel like a worksheet.
- Core activity: invent a product, compose an original advertisement, set and reason about price, select audience and placement strategy, progress through AIDA, publish to a class market, then spend a fixed wallet on other teams' products.
- Studio role: a self-contained, Canva-like creative surface inside the game. It includes a large local asset catalogue, modular product builder, drawing/text/logo tools, pricing feedback, audience information, paired-role prompts, and optional built-in image generation.
- Product assets are deliberately unbranded, mostly monochrome illustrated pieces that students colour and combine.
- Important viewports: 1366×768 classroom laptops and 1920×1080 projectors/desktops. The screenshots show both after a student has named the product, placed a modular tumbler with lid, and added words.
- Accessibility: readable at normal zoom, keyboard operable, visible focus, adequate contrast, and no dependence on colour alone.

## Review questions

1. Is the visual hierarchy immediately understandable to a 15-year-old without teacher explanation?
2. Does the interface feel playful, professional, and appropriate to an advertising/creative-market game?
3. Is the canvas given the right prominence while keeping product construction, audience strategy, price, AIDA, layers, and assets discoverable?
4. Does the layout work at both supplied viewport sizes without illegible scaling, clipped tools, excessive nested scrolling, or wasted space?
5. Are the placed modular product and text visually coherent enough to support good-looking student outcomes?
6. Can a pair understand what to do next and see progress without the UI turning into a task sheet?
7. What responsive information architecture and interaction model should replace or refine the current one?

Return only a concise Opus synthesis containing:

- terminal verdict: PASS or FAIL;
- ranked release-blocking findings grounded in visible evidence;
- strengths worth preserving;
- one concrete responsive redesign specification (not multiple vague options), including regions, sizing/behaviour at 1366×768 and 1920×1080, progressive disclosure, typography, colour/visual identity, and canvas/product composition treatment;
- measurable visual acceptance checks for the next build.

Do not assume a preferred verdict or redesign. Do not rely on any prior review. Do not discuss implementation effort unless it changes the recommended product design.
